The format of your resume determines how recruiters and ATS software read your career story. Choose the right one, and your experience flows logically and your strengths stand out. Choose the wrong one, and you're making the reader work too hard — or worse, the ATS can't parse it at all.

There are three main resume formats, and each works best for different situations. Here's how to choose the right one, with examples and templates for each.

The 3 Resume Formats

1. Reverse-Chronological (Most Common — Use This by Default)

The reverse-chronological format lists your work experience starting with your most recent job and working backward. It's the most widely used format, the most familiar to recruiters, and the most ATS-friendly.

Structure:

  1. Contact Information
  2. Professional Summary
  3. Work Experience (most recent first)
  4. Education
  5. Skills
  6. Certifications (optional)

Best for:

  • Most job seekers in most situations
  • People with a steady career progression
  • Anyone staying in the same industry or field
  • Situations where your recent experience is your strongest selling point

Why it works:

  • Recruiters expect it — they know exactly where to look for what they need
  • ATS parses it cleanly — the format maps directly to how screening software categorizes information
  • It shows career progression naturally — promotions, increasing responsibility, and growth are immediately visible

2. Functional (Skills-Based)

The functional format organizes your resume around skill categories rather than job history. Instead of listing jobs chronologically, you group your accomplishments by skill area (e.g., "Project Management," "Data Analysis," "Client Relations").

Structure:

  1. Contact Information
  2. Professional Summary
  3. Skills Categories (with achievements under each)
  4. Work History (brief — just titles, companies, dates)
  5. Education

Best for:

  • Career changers whose previous job titles don't match their target role
  • People with significant employment gaps they want to de-emphasize
  • Freelancers or consultants with varied project work

Why it's risky:

  • Many ATS platforms struggle to parse functional formats correctly — skills and achievements may not get mapped to specific jobs
  • Recruiters are often suspicious of functional resumes because they can hide gaps or lack of relevant experience
  • It breaks the "show me where you did this" expectation that hiring managers have

Our recommendation: Unless you have a compelling reason to use this format (major career change, 2+ year gap), avoid it. A well-crafted reverse-chronological resume with a strong summary can address career pivots and gaps without sacrificing ATS compatibility.

3. Combination (Hybrid)

The combination format blends elements of both: it leads with a skills/qualifications section, then follows with a reverse-chronological work history. You get to highlight your most relevant skills up front while still showing your career timeline.

Structure:

  1. Contact Information
  2. Professional Summary
  3. Core Competencies / Key Skills (highlighted section)
  4. Work Experience (reverse-chronological)
  5. Education
  6. Certifications

Best for:

  • Senior professionals with deep expertise worth highlighting
  • Career changers who have transferable skills and some relevant experience
  • People whose skills are more impressive than their job titles suggest

How to Choose: Decision Guide

Your Situation Best Format Why
Steady career in the same field Reverse-chronological Shows clear progression
Career change with transferable skills Combination Highlights skills while showing work history
Significant employment gap (2+ years) Combination or Reverse-chronological with strong summary Functional raises red flags
Recent graduate / entry-level Reverse-chronological Simple, clean, expected
Senior executive (15+ years) Combination Leads with strategic expertise
Freelancer / consultant Combination Groups project work by skill area
Applying through online portals (ATS) Reverse-chronological Highest ATS parse rate

When in doubt, use reverse-chronological. It works for 90%+ of job seekers and has the highest ATS compatibility.

Resume Formatting Rules for 2026

Regardless of which format you choose, these rules apply:

Length

  • Entry-level to mid-career (0-10 years): 1 page
  • Senior / executive (10+ years): 2 pages maximum
  • Academic or research roles: CV format, length varies

One-page resumes aren't a hard rule, but they force you to prioritize your strongest content. If everything on your second page is weaker than page one, cut it.

Margins

  • 0.5 to 1 inch on all sides
  • Don't go below 0.5 inches — it makes the document feel cramped and some printers clip the edges

Font Size

  • Name: 16-20pt
  • Section headers: 13-16pt
  • Body text: 10-12pt
  • Minimum readable size: 10pt (going smaller hurts readability and some ATS parsers)

White Space

Leave breathing room between sections. A resume that's crammed edge-to-edge with text is harder to scan quickly, and recruiters spend an average of 6-7 seconds on the first pass. White space guides their eyes to the most important content.

Consistency

Whatever formatting choices you make, apply them consistently:

  • If you bold your job titles, bold all of them
  • If you use month/year date format, use it everywhere
  • If you capitalize section headers, capitalize all of them
  • If you use bullet points in one job entry, use them in all entries

Inconsistency signals carelessness — exactly the wrong impression.

ATS Formatting Essentials

Your resume's format affects whether ATS can read it correctly. Key rules:

  • Single-column layout. Multi-column designs cause content to be read out of order.
  • No tables, text boxes, or graphics. ATS reads text sequentially — these elements break the flow or get skipped.
  • Standard section headers. "Work Experience" not "My Professional Journey."
  • No headers or footers for critical info. Many ATS systems ignore header/footer content.
  • Save as .docx for maximum compatibility. PDF works for most modern systems but not all.
  • Standard bullet characters. Use • or - only, no custom symbols.

FAQ

Is there a "best" resume format?

For most people, reverse-chronological. It's what recruiters expect, what ATS parses most reliably, and what naturally shows career progression. Use combination only when you have a specific reason to lead with skills.

Should I use a resume template?

Yes, if it follows ATS-friendly formatting rules (single column, standard fonts, no graphics or tables). A good template saves time and ensures consistency. A bad template — especially overly designed ones — can hurt your ATS score.

How far back should my work history go?

10-15 years for most roles. For executive positions, you may go further if early career roles are relevant. For entry-level, include internships and relevant academic projects.

Do I need a different format for different industries?

Generally no. Reverse-chronological works across industries. The content changes (a software engineer's resume looks different from a nurse's), but the format stays the same. Creative industries (design, advertising) sometimes accept more visual formats, but only when you're not applying through ATS.

What about one-page vs. two-page resumes?

If you have less than 10 years of experience, aim for one page. If you have 10+ years with significant, relevant accomplishments, two pages is fine. Never go to three pages unless you're writing an academic CV.
