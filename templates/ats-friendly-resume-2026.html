You spent hours on your resume. You tailored it to the job. You even had a friend proofread it. And you still didn't get a callback.

Here's what probably happened: your resume never reached a human. An Applicant Tracking System — ATS — filtered it out before a recruiter ever saw it.

Over 75% of resumes get rejected by ATS before a person reads them. Not because the candidates aren't qualified, but because their resumes aren't formatted for the software that screens them first.

An ATS-friendly resume fixes that. It's a resume designed to be read correctly by automated screening systems while still looking professional to the humans who eventually review it.

Here's exactly how to build one.

What Makes a Resume "ATS-Friendly"?

An ATS-friendly resume is one that automated screening software can:

  1. Parse correctly — extract your name, contact info, job titles, dates, skills, and education into the right data fields
  2. Read in order — follow the content from top to bottom without getting confused by columns, tables, or graphics
  3. Match keywords — find the skills, qualifications, and terms the employer is looking for

It's not about dumbing down your resume. It's about making sure the information you worked hard to include actually gets through to the other side.

ATS-Friendly Resume Format: The Rules

Use a Single-Column Layout

This is the most important formatting rule. ATS software reads documents left to right, top to bottom — like reading a book. Multi-column layouts, sidebar sections, and text boxes cause content to be read out of order or skipped entirely.

Do this:

  • One column, full width
  • Clear section breaks between categories
  • Consistent alignment throughout

Not this:

  • Two-column designs with a skills sidebar
  • Info boxes or floating text elements
  • Creative layouts with content in multiple zones

Choose Standard, Readable Fonts

Stick with fonts that every system can render correctly:

  • Safe choices: Arial, Calibri, Garamond, Georgia, Helvetica, Times New Roman, Cambria
  • Avoid: Custom fonts, decorative fonts, or anything that requires a special download

Font size should be 10-12pt for body text and 13-16pt for section headers. Don't go smaller than 10pt — some parsers struggle with tiny text.

Use Standard Section Headers

ATS software looks for specific section names to categorize your information. Creative headers confuse the parser and your content may end up in the wrong field — or nowhere.

Use these headers:

  • Professional Summary (or Summary)
  • Work Experience (or Professional Experience)
  • Education
  • Skills
  • Certifications
  • Projects
  • Volunteer Experience

Avoid these:

  • "About Me"
  • "My Expertise"
  • "Career Narrative"
  • "What I've Done"
  • "The Highlights"

Stick with Standard Bullet Points

Use simple round bullets (•) or hyphens (-). Avoid:

  • Custom symbols or icons
  • Checkmarks, arrows, or stars
  • Emoji

These can render as garbled characters or blank spaces in ATS systems.

Save as .docx or .pdf

  • .docx — the safest format, universally parsed by every major ATS
  • .pdf — works with most modern ATS platforms (Greenhouse, Lever, Workday), but older systems like Taleo can struggle
  • Avoid: .pages, .odt, image-based PDFs, or Google Docs links

If the job application specifies a format, use that format. If it doesn't, .docx is the safest bet.

Put Contact Info in the Document Body

Many ATS platforms ignore headers and footers. If your name, email, phone number, or LinkedIn URL is in the header or footer, the system might not capture it.

Put all contact information at the top of the main document body.

ATS-Friendly Resume Template

Here's a clean structure that works with every major ATS:

[YOUR FULL NAME] [City, State] | [Phone] | [Email] | [LinkedIn URL] PROFESSIONAL SUMMARY 2-3 sentences summarizing your experience, key skills, and what you bring to the target role. Include 2-3 high-priority keywords from the job description. WORK EXPERIENCE [Job Title] [Company Name] | [City, State] | [Start Date - End Date] - Achievement-focused bullet point with quantified results - Another accomplishment using keywords from the job description - Specific contribution with measurable impact [Job Title] [Company Name] | [City, State] | [Start Date - End Date] - Achievement-focused bullet point with quantified results - Another accomplishment with relevant keywords - Specific contribution with measurable impact EDUCATION [Degree], [Major] [University Name] | [Graduation Year] SKILLS [Skill 1] | [Skill 2] | [Skill 3] | [Skill 4] | [Skill 5] | [Skill 6] | [Skill 7] | [Skill 8] | [Skill 9] | [Skill 10] CERTIFICATIONS [Certification Name], [Issuing Organization] | [Year]

This template is intentionally simple. It works because ATS can parse every section correctly, and it still looks clean and professional to human reviewers.

How to Optimize Your ATS-Friendly Resume

Having the right format is step one. Here's how to optimize the content:

1. Mirror the Job Description's Language

ATS matches your resume against the job posting's keywords. If the job says "project management" and you wrote "managed projects," some systems won't connect them.

Read the job description carefully and use the same terminology:

  • "Data analysis" not "analyzed data"
  • "Cross-functional collaboration" not "worked with different departments"
  • "Agile methodology" not "flexible approach"

Include both the full term and common abbreviations: "Customer Relationship Management (CRM)"

2. Include a Dedicated Skills Section

Even if your skills are demonstrated throughout your experience section, a dedicated Skills section gives the ATS a clean, concentrated block of keywords to scan.

Organize by category if you have many skills:

  • Technical: Python, SQL, Tableau, Google Analytics, Salesforce
  • Methodologies: Agile, Scrum, Six Sigma, Lean
  • Soft Skills: Cross-functional leadership, stakeholder management, strategic planning

3. Quantify Your Achievements

Numbers stand out to both ATS algorithms and human reviewers:

  • Weak: "Improved customer satisfaction"
  • Strong: "Increased customer satisfaction scores from 72% to 91% over 6 months, reducing churn by 18%"
  • Weak: "Managed social media accounts"
  • Strong: "Grew LinkedIn following from 3,200 to 28,000 in 12 months, generating 340 qualified leads"

Use dollar amounts, percentages, time frames, and team sizes whenever possible.

4. Tailor for Every Application

A single generic resume typically matches 40-60% of keywords for any given job. A tailored resume can hit 80-95%.

You don't need to rewrite everything. For each application:

  • Adjust your summary to reflect the specific role
  • Reorder skills to prioritize what the job emphasizes
  • Tweak 2-3 bullet points to include the specific tools or methodologies mentioned
  • Add or remove certifications based on what's listed as preferred

Pro tip: Keep a master resume with all your experience, then create tailored versions by pulling the most relevant content for each application.

What to Avoid in an ATS-Friendly Resume

Tables and Text Boxes

ATS reads content sequentially. Tables cause information to be read across rows instead of within logical sections. A table with your contact info might get parsed as: "John Smith New York john@email.com 555-1234" with no field mapping.

Images, Logos, and Graphics

ATS is text-based. Your company logo, headshot, skill bar charts, and icons are invisible to the parser. Any information conveyed only through graphics is lost.

Headers and Footers

Many ATS platforms skip headers and footers entirely. Never put critical information (name, contact details, page numbers with context) in these areas.

Fancy File Names

Name your file clearly: FirstName-LastName-Resume.docx. Avoid special characters, spaces, or generic names like resume_final_v3 (1).docx.

White Text Keyword Stuffing

Some people try to game ATS by hiding keywords in white text. Modern ATS detects this, and many flag it as a red flag. Even if it passes the software, it's a career-ending move if a recruiter catches it.

FAQ

What's the difference between ATS-friendly and ATS-optimized?

ATS-friendly means your resume can be correctly parsed — the formatting doesn't break the system. ATS-optimized goes further: it means your content is strategically matched to the job description's keywords and requirements to score as high as possible. You need both.

Do I need a different resume for every job?

You don't need a completely different resume, but you should tailor the summary, skills order, and key bullet points for each application. The core structure and most of your experience stays the same.

Can I still make my resume look good?

Absolutely. ATS-friendly doesn't mean ugly. Clean fonts, consistent formatting, strategic use of bold for headers, and well-organized sections create a professional document that works for both ATS and humans. You're giving up decorative elements (graphics, multi-column layouts), not professionalism.

How do I know if my resume is ATS-friendly?

The most reliable way is to test it. Resumatic AI scores your resume against ATS parsing criteria and shows you exactly what's working and what needs to change. You can also do a quick self-check: can you select and copy all text in your document? If yes, ATS can probably read it. If any content is embedded in images or graphics, it can't.

Which ATS platforms should I optimize for?

The major ones are Workday, Greenhouse, Lever, iCIMS, and Taleo. The good news: a single-column, cleanly formatted .docx with strong keyword matching works well across all of them. You don't need to optimize for each platform separately.
