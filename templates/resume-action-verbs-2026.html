The difference between a resume that gets callbacks and one that gets ignored often comes down to word choice. Hiring managers and ATS algorithms both respond better to strong, specific action verbs than to weak, passive language.

"Responsible for managing a team" tells a recruiter nothing about your impact. "Led a 12-person engineering team that shipped 4 product releases in 6 months" tells them everything.

Here are 240+ action verbs and power words organized by category, plus guidance on how to use them effectively.

Why Action Verbs Matter for Your Resume

Two reasons:

  1. ATS keyword matching. Applicant Tracking Systems scan for specific action verbs associated with job requirements. "Managed," "developed," "analyzed," and "implemented" are among the most commonly matched terms in job descriptions. Using the right verbs improves your keyword score.
  2. Human impact. Recruiters spend an average of 6-7 seconds on an initial resume scan. Strong verbs at the start of each bullet point immediately communicate what you did and how well you did it. Weak verbs ("helped," "assisted," "was responsible for") dilute your impact.

How to Choose the Right Action Verb

Match the Job Description

If the job posting says "manage cross-functional teams," use "managed" rather than "oversaw" or "led." ATS algorithms match specific terms, and mirroring the job description's language improves your keyword score.

Match the Seniority Level

  • Entry-level: Assisted, Supported, Contributed, Participated, Completed
  • Mid-level: Managed, Developed, Implemented, Analyzed, Coordinated
  • Senior/Executive: Directed, Spearheaded, Transformed, Pioneered, Drove

Avoid Repetition

Don't start every bullet point with "Managed." Vary your verbs across bullets and across job entries. If you managed people, budgets, and projects, use "Led" for people, "Managed" for budgets, and "Delivered" for projects.

Start Every Bullet with a Verb

Your bullet points should begin with an action verb, not a noun or article:

  • Yes: "Reduced customer onboarding time by 60% through automated email sequences"
  • No: "The customer onboarding process was reduced by 60%"
  • No: "I was responsible for reducing customer onboarding time"
